## 2. Summaries for non-experts

**One line (17 words):**
AntiFLipper helps many devices train one AI model together while spotting devices that secretly feed it mislabeled data.

**Two sentences:**
In federated learning, many devices train a shared AI model without sending their private data, so a few dishonest devices can quietly corrupt it by training on deliberately wrong labels. AntiFLipper catches them cheaply: each device reports how well the shared model fits its data, and the server judges that report against what an honest device holding the same kinds of data should score.

**One paragraph:**
Federated learning lets phones, hospitals or sensors train one shared model without pooling their private data; the central server only sees model updates. That privacy also creates a weakness: a device can train on data whose labels it has deliberately swapped (say, calling every "cat" a "dog"), and the server cannot easily tell. An old, cheap defense asks every device how well the current model does on its own data, then distrusts devices that report low scores. This work shows two flaws in that idea:
- **Unlucky honest devices get punished.** Honest devices that happen to hold harder kinds of data also score low. When data is very uneven across devices, the old defense ended up excluding 18.3 of 20 honest devices on average.
- **Liars get through.** A dishonest device can simply lie about its score.

AntiFLipper fixes the first flaw by comparing each device's score with the score expected for the specific mix of data it holds. It fixes part of the second flaw by also checking the update itself. On a standard image benchmark where 40% of the devices are dishonest, it reached 72.0% accuracy (higher than all six other methods tested) and removed every dishonest device without removing an honest one. One case remains unsolved: dishonest devices that lie about their scores when data is very uneven.

Sources: [C]:33, [C]:47, [N]:2, [N]:27, `table_main.tex`:21.

## 3. The problem

**The attack: label flipping.**
- A malicious client relabels its own data and runs the unmodified training code ([C]:43).
- In this paper the relabeling is a fixed class swap: 0↔1, 2↔3, …, 8↔9 ([C]:72).
- Full flip: every sample in every round. Partial flip: a random fraction, redrawn each round ([C]:72).
- Up to 40% of clients may be malicious ([C]:72; this cap is your decision, [MH]:45).

**Why it is hard to detect.** Under non-IID (skewed) data, honest clients' updates also look unusual, so "unusual" is not evidence of attack ([C]:45).

**Why existing defenses fall short.** Every row below is a claim of the manuscript, not a re-measurement.

| Family | Defenses | Shortcoming stated in the manuscript | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need clean server data | **FLTrust**, **HSCS** | "score clients against clean data at the server, which may be unavailable or unrepresentative" | [C]:45, [C]:62 |
| Treat dissimilarity as suspicious; struggle under non-IID | **Median, Trimmed Mean, Multi-Krum, Bulyan, FoolsGold, FLAME, Tolpegin et al., LFighter** | "All of these treat dissimilar updates as suspicious, yet under non-IID data honest updates are dissimilar too" | [C]:62 |
| Expensive at the server | **Multi-Krum** O(n²W); **FLAME** clusters n² similarities in dimension W; **FLTrust** trains on server data every round | Complexity comparison | [C]:163 |
| Inapplicable at this scale | **Bulyan** needs N ≥ 4f+3; violated by N=20, f=8 | | [C]:62, [C]:175 |
| Accuracy feedback (BaFFLe, data-quality schemes) | | "generally read a low score as evidence of poisoning", which penalizes honest clients with hard classes and can be faked | [C]:47, [C]:64 |

**Measured server cost of the baselines** (matched benchmark, 20 identical updates, one CPU) [M]:
- Multi-Krum 4109 ms, FLAME 3465 ms, FLTrust 1269 ms, LFighter 214 ms, FedAvg 183 ms (`table_cost.tex`:13-17, :22).

**Measured non-IID failures in this paper's own runs** [M]:
- On CIFAR-10 at moderate skew (α_D=1), LFighter found 0.46 of the attackers and FLAME 0.42 ([N]:14-15).
- Multi-Krum's accuracy varies ±15.8 across seeds ([N]:9).
- The reference FLTrust flags 0.44 of honest clients ([N]:10) and had non-finite losses in 6 rounds across 3 of its 3 runs ([N]:67-68; [C]:187).

## 4. The method in plain words

**What each client does (M1).**
- Before training, the client runs the current global model once over its own data. It records its overall accuracy and, for every class, how many samples it holds and how many the model got right ([C]:92).
- That is 84 bytes for 10 classes, by the formula 4+8K bytes [E] ([C]:92).
- It then trains normally and sends the update plus this short report ([C]:70).

**What the server does.**
1. **M2, class-conditional residual.**
   - The server pools per-class accuracy over all clients, weighting each client by its current trust.
   - It computes the accuracy *expected* for each client's own mix of classes and subtracts that from the reported accuracy ([C]:94-100, eq. at [C]:96-97).
   - An honest client with hard classes scores low but is also *expected* to score low, so its residual stays near zero. A flipper scores low in every class it holds, so its residual is negative ([C]:100).
2. **M3, trust update.** Trust moves by the squared deviation of the residual from the mean (η=0.1), then is renormalized ([C]:102-107, [C]:177).
3. **M4, update-consistency check.**
   - The server compares each client's last-layer change with the coordinate-wise median of all clients' last-layer changes (cosine similarity, robust z-score, cut-off z=3).
   - A client far below the others is marked *inconsistent*. Its residual is replaced by the worst of the round, and it receives a flag ([C]:109).
   - This check looks at what the client *did*, not what it *said*.
4. **M6, aggregation.** The new global model is the trust-weighted average of the non-excluded clients' models ([C]:113).

**How a client gets excluded (M5).**
- After 2 warm-up rounds, a client gets a flag in any round in which either:
  - its trust falls below 5% of the uniform share (n_t·τ_j < ρ, ρ=0.05), or
  - the consistency check marks it inconsistent.
- After **6 flags** (c_max=6; flags accumulate and are not reset) the client is excluded.
- At most ⌊0.4N⌋ clients can ever be excluded, the bound of the threat model. If more qualify, the lowest-trust clients go first.
- Sources: [C]:111, [C]:177. The cumulative, non-resetting counter is described for the original code in `Note/updates.md`:27-29.

**The key idea.** Ask not "is this client's accuracy low?" but "is it lower than expected *for the classes this client holds*?" ([C]:49). Then back the report with one cheap check of the update, so that a convincing report alone is not enough ([C]:49, [C]:83). Each signal covers the other's failure ([C]:227):
- The residual protects skewed honest clients.
- The update check catches clients that fake their reports.

## 7. Limitations and open problems

**Stated in the manuscript** ([C]:248):
- Report faking under strong skew is unsolved: 1 of 24 attackers detected ([N]:89-90).
  - Accuracy there is 43.1% vs FedAvg 43.8% ([N]:109, :121).
  - FPR is 0.06, so some honest clients are excluded too (`table_adaptive.tex`:16).
- Cause: the per-class reference includes the scored client. Where attackers hold most of a class, the reference absorbs their deficit ([C]:152, [C]:220).
  - The residual drops the faked-report AUC from 0.64–0.73 (raw) to 0.46–0.58 [M] ([N]:134-137).
- An attacker that aligns its last-layer update with the median could evade CC; colluding attackers were not evaluated.
- Under strong skew with honest reporting, detection was incomplete in 1 of 3 runs.
- Without attackers under strong skew, AntiFLipper is less accurate than undefended FedAvg; combining trust with sample weighting is untested.
- No convergence guarantee ([C]:161).
- Per-class counts reveal label histograms unless a secure-aggregation variant is deployed; that variant is **not implemented** ([C]:165).
- Only synchronous image classification. Partial participation was tested at one rate with uniform sampling. Data-dependent dropout, asynchronous updates, other domains and edge hardware remain untested.
- Three seeds support means and standard deviations, not fine-grained significance claims.

**Also shown by the data or the review, not all stated as limitations:**
- High variance under strong skew with attackers (±11.9), and only 2/3 seed wins over sample-weighted FedAvg ([N]:40, :123).
- Partial flips give no accuracy gain over FedAvg ([C]:216).
- Small scale: 2 image datasets, 20 clients for CIFAR-10 ([C]:171).
- The margin over the reference FLTrust is 72.0 vs 69.2 ([N]:2, :4). Baselines vary strongly across seeds (LFighter ±13.3, Multi-Krum ±15.8), and the two FLTrust implementations differ by 11.4 points (69.2 vs 57.8). Reviewers may question the baseline implementations.
- The conclusion ([C]:254) currently overstates the scope of the accuracy lead; an edit is pending ([MH]:423).
- Client overhead is reported for CIFAR-10 only. MNIST overhead of the original design was 24% ([MH]:143).

## 8. Future directions (for the SoP)

These are follow-ups the work opens. They are not results.

1. **Heterogeneity-aware anomaly detection.**
   - General principle: judge a participant against what *its own data* predicts, not against the population.
   - This applies beyond label flipping, e.g. to free-riders and noisy-label clients.
   - Builds on Props. 1–2 ([C]:141-150).
2. **Fixing self-inclusion bias.**
   - Leave-one-out or robust per-class references, and a theory of when a client can hide in its own reference.
   - The journal version already states a formal result (Prop. 4, Cor. 1; [J]:249-258).
   - This targets the 1-of-24 failure directly.
3. **Distinguishing label noise from poisoning.**
   - Honest clients with noisy labels look like partial flippers.
   - A principled detection boundary is open; the partial-flip results ([C]:216) are a starting point.
4. **Privacy-preserving verification.**
   - Implement and analyze the secure-aggregation variant, in which the server sees only one scalar per client ([C]:165).
   - Quantify leakage over repeated rounds (a GLOBECOM reviewer request, `Paper/v8/reviews.txt`:126).
5. **Adaptive and colluding adversaries.**
   - Attackers that fake reports *and* align their updates, or coordinate class ownership under skew ([C]:248).
   - A natural game-theoretic or robust-statistics direction.
6. **Convergence with time-varying trust weights.**
   - No guarantee exists yet ([C]:161).
7. **Realistic federations.**
   - Cross-device scale, data-dependent dropout, asynchronous FL, and natural partitions (e.g. FEMNIST, UCI HAR; [MH]:146).
   - On-device energy and latency measurements on edge hardware.
8. **Combining trust with sample weighting.**
   - Sample-weighted FedAvg wins without attackers under strong skew ([C]:206).
   - Untested.
